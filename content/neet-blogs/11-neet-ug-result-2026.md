## How to Download Your NEET UG 2026 Scorecard

The process is short. The mistakes people make around it are not.

1. Go to **neet.nta.nic.in**
2. Click on the link that reads **"NEET (UG) 2026 Result"**
3. Enter your **Application Number**, **Date of Birth**, and the **Security Pin** shown on screen
4. Click **Submit**
5. Your scorecard loads on screen
6. **Download the PDF and take at least two printouts**

That last step is not filler advice. The NTA scorecard portal stays open for a limited window, and MCC and every state counselling authority will ask for the scorecard at document verification. Students who lose the PDF in September end up filing grievance requests during the exact week they should be locking choices.

**Save it in three places:** your phone, your email (mail it to yourself), and a cloud drive. Name the file something you can find under pressure — `NEET2026_Scorecard_YourName_RollNo.pdf`.

| 1 (joint) | **Panshul Bansal** | Haryana | 715 |

**Why 715 and not 720?** A perfect 720 requires all 180 questions correct. At 715, the candidate has effectively conceded a single question (–1 negative on one wrong answer against a 720 baseline yields 715). At this altitude, tie-breaking is decided by NTA's published policy: higher marks in Biology, then Chemistry, then the lowest proportion of incorrect answers, and finally age. Both toppers here are reported as joint AIR 1, meaning the tie-break criteria did not separate them at the reporting level.

Candidates at this score band are effectively choosing between [AIIMS Delhi](/neet-colleges/all-india-institute-of-medical-sciences-new-delhi-125-7-seats-are-earmarked-for-indian-nationals-and-07-seats-are-earmarked-for-foreign-under-self-finance) and nothing else — AIIMS Delhi's MBBS closing rank for General AIQ has historically sat inside the top 60.

## NEET UG 2026 Qualifying Cutoff Explained

Here is the single most misunderstood thing about NEET.

**The qualifying cutoff is not the admission cutoff.**

The qualifying cutoff is a percentile threshold, fixed by regulation, that determines whether your name enters the merit list at all. It has almost nothing to do with whether you get a seat.

### The percentile framework (stable across years)

| Category | Qualifying Percentile |
|---|---|
| General / EWS | 50th percentile |
| OBC-NCL | 40th percentile |
| SC | 40th percentile |
| ST | 40th percentile |
| General-PwD | 45th percentile |
| OBC / SC / ST-PwD | 40th percentile |

These percentile requirements are laid down under the regulations of the **National Medical Commission (NMC)** and do not change year to year. What changes is the **marks** that correspond to those percentiles, because that depends on how the cohort performed.

### For historical trend context

For reference, the General category 50th-percentile cutoff has moved substantially across recent years as paper difficulty and cohort size shifted. The direction of travel is what matters for interpretation, not the exact figure: a harder paper compresses the distribution downward and pulls the qualifying marks down with it, while an easier paper pushes it up. Because 11.21 lakh of ~20 lakh qualified in 2026 — a qualification rate of roughly 56%, broadly consistent with recent years — the 2026 cutoff is unlikely to be an outlier.

**What you should actually care about:** the **closing rank** at the colleges you want. That is a different number entirely, published by MCC and state authorities after each counselling round. ## Understanding AIR vs Category Rank vs Percentile

Three numbers, three different jobs. Students conflate them constantly.

**All India Rank (AIR)** — your position among all 20 lakh candidates who appeared. This is the number MCC uses for the **open/unreserved** merit list in All India Quota counselling. Everyone competes on AIR for open seats.

**Category Rank** — your position among candidates of your own category. This is used for **reserved seats** within AIQ and state counselling. An OBC-NCL candidate with AIR 45,000 might have a category rank of 18,000, and both numbers are live simultaneously in different merit lists.

**Percentile** — the percentage of candidates who scored at or below you. Percentile determines *qualification*. It plays no role in seat allotment. A 99.2 percentile sounds spectacular and, against 20 lakh candidates, corresponds to an AIR near 16,000 — which is a completely different conversation from what "99.2 percentile" feels like.

**The practical rule:** for admissions, ignore percentile entirely. Work with AIR and category rank.

## What Happens Immediately After the Result

The result is a starting gun, not a finish line. Here is the sequence:

1. **Download and secure the scorecard** (today)
2. **Check your AIR and category rank** — not your percentile
3. **Run a college prediction** against last year's closing ranks — [do it here](/neet)
4. **Assemble your document set** — this is the step everyone underestimates
5. **Register for MCC AIQ counselling** at mcc.nic.in when the window opens
6. **Register separately for your state counselling** — these are parallel processes, not sequential
7. **Fill and lock choices** — the locking step is where seats are won and lost
8. **Participate in seat allotment rounds**
9. **Complete document verification**
10. **Report to the allotted college** within the deadline

**Steps 5 and 6 run in parallel, and missing either one is unrecoverable.** MCC registration and state counselling registration are separate portals, separate fees, separate deadlines. Every year, thousands of students register for one and assume it covers the other.

## Fraud Warnings from NTA

NTA has explicitly warned candidates about fraudulent calls, fake admission offers and websites claiming guaranteed seats or score improvements. This warning is issued every year because the fraud is real, industrial and targets students in exactly this window.

**Hard rules:**

- **No one can guarantee you a medical seat.** Not for money, not for a "management quota booking," not for anything. Allotment is algorithmic and rank-based.
- **Never share your NEET login credentials.** Not with a coaching institute, not with a "counsellor," not with anyone offering to fill your choices for you.
- **Never pay anyone who calls you claiming to be from MCC or NTA.** Neither body calls candidates asking for money.
- **Only trust these domains:**
  - `neet.nta.nic.in` — result, scorecard, notices
  - `mcc.nic.in` — All India Quota counselling
  - Your official state counselling portal

Legitimate counselling guidance — including ours — helps you understand your options and fill your choice list well. It does not, and cannot, get you a seat your rank does not earn.
