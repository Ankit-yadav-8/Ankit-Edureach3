# Expected NEET Cutoff 2026 for Government Medical Colleges
> **The NEET 2026 qualifying cutoff is the 50th percentile for General/EWS, 45th for General-PwD and 40th for OBC-NCL, SC and ST.** The admission cutoff is different and much higher: government MBBS through All India Quota typically closes around AIR 10,000–15,000 for General, based on previous years' MCC closing ranks.

*Reviewed by the College Parichay counselling desk — updated 17 July 2026. Rank estimates are derived from NTA's official NEET UG 2026 score distribution and previous years' MCC closing ranks.*

## The Two Cutoffs Everyone Confuses

Before a single number: there are **two completely different things** called "NEET cutoff," and mixing them up is the most common and most expensive error a NEET candidate makes.

| | **Qualifying Cutoff** | **Admission Cutoff (Closing Rank)** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A percentile threshold set by regulation | The rank of the last student admitted to a specific college |
| Who sets it | NTA, under NMC regulations | Nobody. It's an *outcome* of counselling. |
| When you know it | Result day — **16 July 2026** | After each counselling round concludes |
| What it means | Whether you're on the merit list | Whether you get a seat |
| Typical General value | ~50th percentile | AIR ~10,000–15,000 for government MBBS |
| Relationship | You must clear this to enter counselling | This is what actually decides your college |

**11.21 lakh candidates cleared the qualifying cutoff in 2026.** There are roughly **56,000 government MBBS seats** in India. The qualifying cutoff told 11.21 lakh people they were eligible. The admission cutoff will tell about 56,000 of them where they're going.

Everything below is about the second number. That's the one that matters.

### The percentile framework (fixed under NMC regulations)

| Category | Qualifying Percentile |
|---|---|
| General / EWS | **50th** |
| General-PwD | **45th** |
| OBC-NCL | **40th** |
| SC | **40th** |
| ST | **40th** |
| OBC / SC / ST-PwD | **40th** |

These percentiles do not change year to year. They are laid down under the regulations of the **National Medical Commission (NMC)**.

### NEET UG 2026 qualifying marks

**What we can say with confidence:** 11.21 lakh of ~20 lakh candidates qualified — a rate of roughly **56%**, broadly consistent with recent years. That stability means the 2026 qualifying cutoff is very unlikely to be an outlier in either direction.

**Why the marks move but the percentile doesn't:** the 50th percentile is defined against the cohort, not against an absolute standard. A harder paper compresses the whole distribution downward and drags the qualifying marks down with it. An easier paper pushes them up. The percentile stays at 50 regardless. This is why "the cutoff went down, so the paper was easy" is exactly backwards.

- At the top, marks are cheap and ranks are expensive. Going from 690 to 700 moves you ~120 ranks.

**If you are between 480 and 580, five marks is the difference between two entirely different colleges.** This is why estimating your position from a coaching institute's rank predictor is not good enough, and why students in this band should map their actual AIR against actual closing ranks.

## How Cutoffs Are Actually Determined

Nobody sets an admission cutoff. It emerges.

Here is the mechanism:

1. All qualified candidates are ranked by AIR (and by category rank within each category)
2. Each candidate submits an ordered choice list
3. The allotment algorithm processes candidates in rank order — best rank first
4. Each candidate gets the highest choice on their list that still has a seat available
5. When a college's seats run out, the rank of the last candidate allotted becomes that college's **closing rank** for that round and category

**The closing rank is a residue, not a target.** It's whatever rank happened to be standing when the seats ran out.

**This is why cutoffs move between years.** The four levers:

| Lever | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Paper difficulty** | Changes the marks-to-rank mapping, not the rank-to-college mapping |
| **Seat matrix changes** | New colleges or seat increases pull closing ranks *down* (worse ranks get in) |
| **Candidate preferences** | If a college becomes fashionable, its closing rank tightens |
| **Number of candidates** | More candidates at the same seat count pushes closing ranks tighter |

**The practical consequence:** compare **ranks to ranks**, never marks to marks. A 600 in 2024 and a 600 in 2026 are different ranks. But AIR 5,000 in 2024 and AIR 5,000 in 2026 buy roughly similar colleges, adjusted for seat matrix changes.

Every "expected cutoff in marks" article you read is doing the wrong arithmetic. Work in ranks.

**Read the OBC-NCL row carefully. This is the most important line in this article for the largest group of readers.**

The practical result: **OBC-NCL closing ranks at good colleges sit much closer to General closing ranks than most students expect** — often within 20–40%, not the 2–3x cushion that gets assumed.

If you are OBC-NCL and planning on the basis of "I'll get in well below the General cutoff," recalibrate now.

## State Quota: Why Your Domicile Changes Everything

**85% of state government college seats are reserved for state domicile candidates.** That's roughly 48,000 of the ~56,000 government MBBS seats in India, versus ~8,000 in AIQ.

You've been reading AIQ numbers. **State quota is six times bigger and you are competing against a state, not a country.**

### The variance is enormous

The same score produces wildly different outcomes depending on domicile, driven by the ratio of a state's NEET candidates to its government seats:

| State type | Example | Effect on your rank's value |
|---|---|---|
| **High candidate-to-seat ratio** | Delhi, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala | State quota barely easier than AIQ |
| **Moderate ratio** | Rajasthan, MP, Gujarat, UP, West Bengal | State quota meaningfully easier |
| **Low ratio** | Most North Eastern states, Ladakh, A&N, Lakshadweep, smaller UTs | State quota dramatically easier |

The official 2026 state topper data makes this concrete: **Ladakh's state topper scored 530.** The national topper scored 715. A 530 in Ladakh's state quota and a 530 in Karnataka's state quota lead to completely different lives.

**This is not a statement about ability.** It's a statement about coaching density, school infrastructure and pool size. And it is why your domicile is the single most valuable asset you hold right now.

### Do this today

If you have any domicile you haven't considered — a state you were born in, schooled in for 7+ years, or where your parents are domiciled — read that state's eligibility rules this week. Rules vary sharply:

- Some states require 7 years of continuous schooling
- Some accept the parent's domicile
- Some accept either
- Some require the Class 10 **and** 12 to be from the state
- A few states (notably Delhi) have their own restrictive definitions

**Thirty minutes on your state counselling authority's information bulletin is the highest-leverage thing available to you right now.** Higher than anything else in this article.

## Deemed and Private: The Other Number

Deemed universities are counselled through **MCC**, not state authorities. Private colleges are counselled through state authorities.

**Deemed university closing ranks are wide** — frequently into the lakhs — because the binding constraint is not rank. It's money.

| | Deemed University MBBS | Private College MBBS |
|---|---|---|
| Counselling | MCC | State authority |
| Typical total fee | ₹80 lakh – ₹1.5 crore | ₹60 lakh – ₹1.2 crore |
| Closing rank (General) | Often 1,50,000+ | Varies widely by state |
| NRI quota | Yes, far wider ranks | Yes |

**The thing to understand:** at deemed universities, the "cutoff" that filters you is the fee, not the rank. If you cannot fund it, the closing rank is irrelevant to you, and time spent researching deemed cutoffs is time not spent on your state quota choice list.

**Before you register:** sit down with your parents and a calculator. Not a conversation — a spreadsheet. Course fee + hostel + mess + interest over the repayment period. Then compare against what a government BDS, a government AYUSH seat, or a considered drop year actually costs. ## Round-Wise Cutoff Movement

Closing ranks are not one number. They move across rounds, and understanding the direction saves seats.

| Round | Typical closing rank behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Round 1** | **Tightest.** Everyone's in the pool, best ranks take the best seats. |
| **Round 2** | **Loosens.** Round 1 candidates who took free exit or joined elsewhere vacate seats. |
| **Round 3** | **Loosens further.** More attrition. |
| **Stray Vacancy** | **Widest — sometimes dramatically.** Leftover seats, small pool. **No exit permitted.** |

**The strategic implications:**

1. **Don't despair at Round 1.** A rank that misses a college in Round 1 frequently clears it in Round 2 or 3. Round 1 closing ranks are the *hardest* version of the number.
2. **Use Round 1 closing ranks to recalibrate.** MCC publishes them. They are the single best input for your Round 2 choice list — better than any prediction, because they're this year's actual data.
3. **The stray round is a trap for the careless.** Ranks are widest, which is tempting. But there is no exit — if you're allotted, you must join. Only enter with choices you will genuinely accept.

## Why Expected Cutoffs Are Estimates, Not Promises

Everything in this article that isn't drawn from NTA's official distribution is an estimate. Here's exactly what that means, because you deserve to know how much weight to put on each number.

**What is official and hard:**
- The qualifying percentile framework (50th/45th/40th) — NMC regulation, doesn't change
- The 2026 score distribution anchor points (700+ → 19; 690 → 138; 650 → 1,492; 600 → 10,160; 500 → 90,780) — published by NTA
- The category-wise qualified counts
- The marks→AIR conversion **at those five anchor points**

**What is interpolated:**
- Every marks→AIR row *between* the anchor points

**What is estimated:**
- Every college closing rank in this article. All of them. These are derived from previous years' MCC data adjusted for the 2026 cohort, and they will be wrong by some margin.

**What will make them wrong:**
- Seat matrix changes (new colleges, seat increases) — these pull closing ranks wider
- Shifts in candidate preference between colleges
- Round-wise movement, which is large

**The only authoritative source for 2026 closing ranks is MCC and your state counselling authority, after each round concludes.** Nothing on this page — or any page, including the ones with more confident-looking tables — is a substitute.

**Use these estimates for one job:** deciding what to research and what to put on your choice list. Do not use them to decide that a college is out of reach and skip listing it. Listing costs nothing. Not listing costs seats.

### What is the NEET 2026 qualifying cutoff?
The 50th percentile for General and EWS, 45th for General-PwD, and 40th for OBC-NCL, SC and ST. These percentiles are fixed under NMC regulations. The corresponding marks ranges were published by NTA with the result on 16 July 2026.

### Is the qualifying cutoff the same as the admission cutoff?
No, and this is the most costly confusion in NEET. The qualifying cutoff decides whether you enter counselling — 11.21 lakh candidates cleared it in 2026. The admission cutoff is a college's closing rank and decides whether you get a seat. There are roughly 56,000 government MBBS seats.

### What rank do I need for government MBBS in 2026?
Through All India Quota, roughly AIR 15,000 or better for General category. Through state quota, it depends entirely on your domicile — the variance between states is enormous.

### What is the expected AIIMS Delhi cutoff for 2026?
Approximately AIR 50–70 for General category. Context: only 19 candidates scored above 700 and 138 scored 690 or above in NEET 2026.

### If I scored 600, what is my rank?
Approximately **AIR 10,160**. This is drawn from NTA's official 2026 score distribution, which reported 10,160 candidates at 600 or above.

### If I scored 650, what is my rank?
Approximately **AIR 1,492** — again from NTA's official distribution.

### Why do NEET cutoffs change every year?
Because closing ranks are an outcome of counselling, not a target. They shift with paper difficulty (which changes the marks-to-rank mapping), seat matrix changes, candidate preferences and cohort size. Always compare ranks to ranks, never marks to marks.

### Is the OBC-NCL cutoff much lower than General?
Less than most students assume. OBC-NCL candidates are 46% of all 2026 qualifiers but hold 27% of AIQ reserved seats — the most oversubscribed category. Expect closing ranks roughly 1.3x–2.5x wider than General, not 3x or more.

### Does state quota have a different cutoff from AIQ?
Yes, and it's usually far more favourable. State quota is 85% of state government seats — roughly 48,000 nationally versus ~8,000 in AIQ — and you compete only against your state's domicile candidates.

### Do cutoffs go down in later rounds?
Yes. Round 1 closing ranks are the tightest. Rounds 2 and 3 loosen as candidates vacate seats, and the stray vacancy round is widest. But the stray round has no exit provision — only enter it with choices you will accept.

### What is the expected government BDS cutoff for 2026?
Roughly AIR 15,000–30,000 for top government dental colleges, extending to 1,20,000+ for lower-tier government BDS in General category.

### What rank do deemed universities require?
Often 1,50,000 or wider. But rank is not the real filter at deemed universities — the ₹80 lakh to ₹1.5 crore fee is. If you can't fund it, the closing rank is irrelevant to your planning.

### My EWS certificate is from FY 2024–25. Is it valid for 2026 counselling?
No. It must be issued for **FY 2025–26** in the central government format. An invalid EWS certificate collapses your claim to General mid-counselling, which at a 1.2x–2x rank multiplier frequently costs a government seat.

### Can I trust expected cutoff articles?
Only as far as they're honest about their basis. The marks-to-rank anchor points in this article come from NTA's official distribution. Every college closing rank here is an estimate from previous years' MCC data and will carry error. The only authoritative 2026 closing ranks come from MCC after each round.

### Should I skip listing a college if my rank is above its expected cutoff?
No. Never. Listing a choice costs nothing — the algorithm simply moves down your list if you're not eligible. Every college you don't list is a seat you cannot be allotted. Expected cutoffs should guide your research, not shorten your choice list.
