# NEET Counseling Process Explained: AIQ, State Quota, Deemed & Every Step (2026)

Here's a scene we witness every single year.

A student clears NEET with a genuinely good score. The family celebrates. Sweets are distributed. And then, roughly two weeks later, that same family is sitting in front of a laptop at 11:47 PM, utterly bewildered, googling *"what is AIQ round 2 free exit"* while a registration deadline ticks down.

The exam was the hard part academically. But counseling is where good scores quietly go to waste — not because the students weren't smart, but because nobody explained the process to them in plain language.

So that's what this guide is. The entire NEET UG counseling process, explained the way a patient older sibling who's done this before would explain it. No jargon dumps. No fear-mongering. Just the map.

## The big picture: counseling is not one process, it's several running in parallel

This is the mental model that unlocks everything. NEET has *one* exam but *multiple* counseling authorities, and they run at overlapping times.

Broadly, you're dealing with:

1. **All India Quota (AIQ)** — conducted centrally by the MCC (Medical Counselling Committee). This covers 15% of state government college seats, plus central institutions, deemed universities, and a few other categories.

2. **State Quota** — conducted separately by *each* state's own counseling authority, covering 85% of that state's government college seats (plus state private colleges, depending on the state).

3. **Deemed & Central Universities** — handled within the MCC process.

4. **Management/NRI quota** — private and deemed college seats at higher fees, filled through their respective processes.

The key insight: **you can — and usually should — participate in more than one of these at the same time.** A student might be in AIQ counseling *and* their home-state counseling simultaneously, keeping both doors open. Understanding this parallelism is half the battle.

## AIQ vs State Quota: which one is "better"?

Neither. They're tools for different jobs.

**All India Quota** ignores your home state — you compete nationally, and you can win a seat anywhere in the country. Great if you're open to relocating and your rank is strong.

**State Quota** gives home-state students a protected pool of 85% of seats, often with lower cutoffs for locals. This is frequently where "mid-range" scorers land their government seat. If your priority is a government seat close to home, your state counseling is often your best friend.

Most well-advised students register for **both**. We break the strategic trade-offs down further in our [government vs private medical college](/govt-vs-private-medical-college) guide, because the quota you choose interacts directly with the fees you'll pay.

## The rounds: how counseling actually unfolds

Counseling happens in a sequence of rounds. The exact names vary by authority and year, but the rhythm is consistent:

### Round 1 & Round 2 — the main event
This is where most seats are allotted. You register, fill and lock your choices, and the system runs a mock/allotment. If you get a seat you're happy with, you take it. If not, you continue.

### Mop-up round
Vacant seats and freshly surrendered seats get reallocated. Sometimes surprisingly good seats open up here because higher-rank candidates left them for something else. This is why patience pays.

### Stray vacancy round
The final clean-up for seats still lying empty. Rules here are stricter — often you can't casually reject a seat you're allotted.

Each round has its own registration window, choice-filling window, and allotment result. Miss a window and you miss the round. This is not an exam authority that waits for you.

## Choice filling: the part that decides everything

If you take one thing from this article, take this: **your choice list is the most powerful lever you have in the entire process, and most students under-use it badly.**

Choice filling means you list the colleges (and specific course-quota combinations) you'd accept, *in order of preference*. The system tries to give you the highest choice on your list that your rank can reach.

Two rules that quietly decide outcomes:

**Fill more choices than you think you need.** Students who fill five choices out of fear end up with no seat when those five don't come through. Students who fill a long, thoughtfully ordered list get *something* — and often something good.

**Order by genuine preference, not by prestige alone.** The system is honest: it gives you your top *reachable* choice. So put what you actually want at the top, then layer realistic options below.

Getting this list right is genuinely hard to do alone at midnight. It's the single thing our [NEET Counseling desk](/neet-counseling) spends the most time on with students — and it's where the most seats are won and lost. ## The money mechanics nobody warns you about

Counseling involves real money moving before you've even confirmed a seat. Come prepared:

- **Registration fee & security deposit.** Both AIQ and many state/deemed processes require a refundable security deposit up front. For deemed universities this can be substantial. Have funds ready — students have lost rounds simply because the deposit wasn't arranged in time.

- **"Free exit" vs "forfeit" windows.** After certain rounds, leaving is free and your deposit comes back. After others, exiting means forfeiting your deposit — or worse, getting barred from later rounds. Knowing *which round you're in* is critical before you click "accept" or "reject."

- **Fee confirmation on allotment.** When you're allotted a seat, you typically pay the tuition/admission fee to lock it, then report physically to the college. Miss the reporting deadline and the seat evaporates.

We keep saying "deadline" — that's not an accident. Counseling is a deadline machine. Put every window in a calendar the day it's announced.

## Documents: get this folder ready now, not later

Nothing derails a good allotment like a missing document at the reporting stage. Keep a physical *and* scanned set of:

- NEET admit card and scorecard/result
- Class 10 & 12 mark sheets and certificates
- Birth/date-of-birth proof
- Photo ID (Aadhaar/passport)
- Category certificate (if applicable) in the exact prescribed format
- Domicile/nativity certificate (crucial for state quota)
- Passport-size photographs
- Any EWS/PwD certificates in the specified format

That category and domicile certificate detail matters more than people realise — a certificate in the wrong format is treated as no certificate at all, and it can knock you out of the reserved seat you qualified for. If category quotas are part of your plan, read [NEET category-wise cutoffs explained](/neet-category-cutoffs).

## Putting it together: a sane sequence to follow

1. **Register for AIQ and your home-state counseling both.** Keep maximum doors open.
2. **Predict realistically first.** Before you fill a single choice, know what your rank can actually reach with our [College Predictor](/neet-college-predictor).
3. **Build a long, honestly-ordered choice list.** Dream choices at top, realistic ones below, safe ones at the bottom. Never leave it thin.
4. **Track every deadline in a calendar.** Registration, choice locking, results, reporting — all of it.
5. **Understand the exit rules of each round before you accept or reject.** Free exit vs forfeit is not a detail.
6. **Keep your document folder reporting-ready from day one.**

## A final word from our desk

Counseling rewards preparation and punishes improvisation. The students who come out with the seat they deserved are almost never the ones who "figured it out as they went." They're the ones who understood the map before the journey started.

You've already cleared the hard exam. Don't let a process you *can* master be the thing that stands between you and your white coat.
