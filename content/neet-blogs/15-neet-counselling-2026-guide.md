# NEET Counselling 2026: Complete MCC & State Guide
> **NEET counselling 2026 runs through two separate processes: MCC All India Quota counselling at mcc.nic.in covering 15% of state government seats plus all central institutions, and state counselling covering the remaining 85%.** Both require separate registration, separate fees and separate deadlines. Candidates must register for both.

*Reviewed by the College Parichay counselling desk — updated 17 July 2026. Round rules change between years and between rounds; the information bulletin for each round is the only authority on that round.*

## How NEET Counselling Actually Works

The [NEET UG 2026 result was declared on 16 July](/blog/neet-ug-result-2026). **11.21 lakh candidates qualified.** India has roughly **56,000 government MBBS seats** and about **1.18 lakh MBBS seats** in total.

Counselling is the machine that maps the first number onto the second. It is not a test, an interview, or a negotiation. It's an algorithm, and it runs on two inputs: **your rank** and **your choice list.**

You cannot change your rank. You have total control over your choice list. **That asymmetry is the entire strategic content of counselling**, and almost every avoidable seat loss in India traces back to a student who optimised nothing about the one input they controlled.

**The core loop, repeated each round:**

1. Register on the portal and pay the fees
2. Fill an ordered list of choices
3. **Lock** the list
4. The algorithm processes candidates in rank order and gives each the highest choice they're eligible for
5. Results publish
6. You accept, exit, upgrade, or forfeit
7. Vacated seats roll into the next round

That's it. Everything below is detail on those seven steps.

## MCC vs State Counselling: The Structural Split

This is the fact everything else depends on. Read the table, then read the paragraph under it.

| | **All India Quota (AIQ)** | **State Quota** |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority** | Medical Counselling Committee (MCC) | State counselling authority |
| **Portal** | `mcc.nic.in` | Varies by state |
| **Covers** | 15% of state government college seats · **100%** of AIIMS, JIPMER, ESIC, AFMC, Central Universities (BHU, AMU, Delhi University), Deemed Universities | 85% of state government college seats · state private colleges |
| **Approx MBBS seats** | ~8,000 government + deemed | ~48,000 government + private |
| **Eligibility** | All qualified candidates, nationwide | Usually requires state **domicile** |
| **Merit basis** | AIR (open) + category rank (reserved) | State merit list derived from NEET marks |
| **Category certificate** | **Central format** | **State format** |
| **Registration** | Separate | Separate |
| **Fee** | Separate | Separate |
| **Deadlines** | Separate | Separate |

**Read the last four rows again, slowly.**

MCC counselling and state counselling are two entirely independent processes running in parallel. Registering for one does not register you for the other. There is no shared login, no shared fee, no shared calendar, and no system that notices you've missed one.

**Every year, thousands of students register for one and assume it covers the other.** They find out in September.

**And notice which is bigger.** State quota is roughly **six times** the size of AIQ in government MBBS terms. Everyone spends July reading about AIQ closing ranks. Most of them will get their seat through state quota.

**Your domicile is the most valuable asset you hold right now.** If you have any domicile you haven't considered — a state you were born in, schooled in for 7+ years, or where your parents are domiciled — read that state's eligibility rules this week. Thirty minutes. Highest-leverage action available to you.

## MCC AIQ Counselling: Full Mechanics

**Portal:** `mcc.nic.in`

### The round structure

| Round | Pool | Free exit? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Round 1** | Everyone | **Yes** | Tightest closing ranks. |
| **Round 2** | Round 1 non-joiners + exits + fresh | Rules vary — read the bulletin | Ranks loosen. |
| **Round 3** | Remaining pool | Rules vary | Ranks loosen further. |
| **Stray Vacancy** | Leftover seats | **NO** | Widest ranks. **Mandatory joining.** |

### What happens across rounds

Closing ranks **loosen** from Round 1 to stray. The mechanism is attrition: candidates who take free exit, join elsewhere, or simply don't report vacate seats that roll into the next round's pool.

**Three consequences worth internalising:**

1. **Round 1 closing ranks are the hardest version of the number.** A rank that misses a college in Round 1 frequently clears it in Round 2 or 3. Do not despair at Round 1.
2. **MCC publishes Round 1 closing ranks.** They are the best possible input for your Round 2 list — better than any prediction, because they're *this year's actual data* rather than last year's adjusted.
3. **The stray round is the widest and the most dangerous.** See its own section below.

**Read each round's information bulletin.** MCC changes rules between years and between rounds. The bulletin for Round 2 of 2026 is the only authority on Round 2 of 2026 — not what your senior told you about 2024, and not this article.

## The Seat Matrix: What's Actually Available

The seat matrix is MCC's published list of every seat, by college, course, category and quota. It publishes before choice filling.

**Read it. Actually read it.** Most students never open it and fill choices from memory and rumour.

| Seat type in AIQ | What it is |
|---|---|
| **AIQ Government** | 15% of state government college seats |
| **Central Institutions** | AIIMS, JIPMER, ESIC, AFMC, BHU, AMU, DU, Central Universities — 100% through MCC |
| **Deemed Universities** | 100% through MCC. High fee. |
| **Central Universities** | Through MCC |
| **ESIC** | Insured Persons quota + open |
| **AFMC** | Separate additional selection process |
| **B.Sc Nursing** | Central institutions, through MCC |

**What the seat matrix tells you that nothing else will:**

- Exactly how many seats exist in your category at each college
- Which colleges are new this year (**new colleges have no closing rank history — they're often the softest seats in the matrix**)
- Which colleges had seat increases (these loosen closing ranks)
- Whether the course you assumed exists actually does

**The new-college point is the actionable one.** A college added to the matrix this year has no historical closing rank, so no predictor covers it and most students skip it. That's precisely why it closes wide. Read the matrix, find the new entries, list them.

### AIQ reservation

| Category | AIQ Reservation |
|---|---|
| OBC-NCL | 27% |
| SC | 15% |
| ST | 7.5% |
| EWS | 10% |
| PwBD | 5% (horizontal) |

### The arithmetic nobody publishes

Set the reservation against the official 2026 qualified counts:

| Category | Qualified 2026 | Share of Qualifiers | AIQ Reservation |
|---|---|---|---|
| General | 2.91 lakh | 26% | — |
| **OBC-NCL** | **5.12 lakh** | **46%** | **27%** |
| SC | 1.59 lakh | 14% | 15% |
| ST | 63,716 | 6% | 7.5% |
| Gen-EWS | 95,026 | 8% | 10% |
| PwBD | 3,666 | 0.3% | 5% (horizontal) |

**OBC-NCL candidates are 46% of all qualifiers and hold 27% of AIQ reserved seats.** That is the most oversubscribed category in Indian medical admissions by a wide margin.

**The practical consequence:** OBC-NCL closing ranks at good colleges sit much closer to General than students expect — often within 1.3x to 2.5x, not the 3x+ cushion that gets assumed in coaching-centre folklore. If you're OBC-NCL and planning on the basis of a large cushion, recalibrate today.

**SC and ST candidates have a genuinely favourable ratio** (14% of qualifiers for 15% of seats; 6% for 7.5%). Closing ranks typically run 4x–8x and 6x–12x wider than General respectively.

### Horizontal vs vertical reservation

**Vertical** (SC/ST/OBC/EWS) — separate merit lists, separate seat pools.
**Horizontal** (PwBD) — cuts *across* all vertical categories. A PwBD candidate competes within their own vertical category's PwBD sub-quota.

This distinction confuses people constantly. A SC-PwBD candidate is in the SC list, competing for the PwBD seats within it.

**Everyone competes on AIR for open seats regardless of category.** If your AIR clears the open cutoff, you take an open seat and free your category seat for someone else. This is automatic. You don't choose it.

## Special Quotas Most Students Miss

Free money, structurally. Almost nobody checks.

| Quota | Who's eligible | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **ESIC — Insured Persons (IP)** | Children of ESIC-insured workers | ESIC colleges via MCC |
| **AFMC** | Meets AFMC criteria + separate selection | AFMC Pune via MCC |
| **NRI** | NRI/OCI candidates | Deemed + private. Far wider ranks, far higher fees. |
| **Wards of Defence Personnel** | Varies by state | State counselling |
| **Government school quota** | Varies by state (notably Tamil Nadu's 7.5%) | State counselling |
| **PwBD** | Certified from a designated centre | Both |
| **Jammu & Kashmir migrant** | As notified | State counselling |
| **Single girl child** | A few states | State counselling |

**The ESIC-IP one is worth five minutes of your time.** If either parent is an ESIC-insured worker — and lakhs of Indian families are without thinking of it as a quota — the IP quota closing ranks are *substantially* wider than open ESIC seats. Ask your parents whether their employer deducts ESIC. It takes one question.

**The state-specific ones are where the real variance lives.** Tamil Nadu's 7.5% government school quota, various defence quotas, various regional quotas — these are in your **state counselling authority's information bulletin**, and each state's list is different.

**Read your state's bulletin cover to cover.** It is a boring 60-page PDF. It is also the highest information-density document in this entire process, and reading it puts you ahead of roughly 90% of your competition.

## The Certificate Format Trap

This section exists because it is the single most common unforced error in Indian medical counselling, and almost no content covers it.

### Category certificate: central vs state format

**They are different documents.**

| | AIQ (MCC) | State counselling |
|---|---|---|
| Required format | **Central government prescribed format** | **State format** |
| Issued by | Competent authority, central format | State competent authority |
| Valid for the other? | **No** | **No** |

A state-format OBC-NCL certificate is **valid for state counselling and invalid for AIQ**. If you show up to MCC document verification with a state-format certificate, your category claim collapses to General on the spot.

**If you only hold the state one, apply for the central one today.** Two to four weeks in most districts. MCC will not wait, will not accept an application receipt, and will not make an exception.

### EWS: the financial year trap

The EWS certificate must be:
- **Central government format**
- Issued for financial year **2025–26**

An EWS certificate from FY 2024–25 is invalid. It gets rejected at verification, your EWS claim collapses to General, and at a 1.2x–2x rank multiplier that collapse is frequently the exact difference between a government seat and none.

**Check the financial year on your certificate right now.** This paragraph has probably saved more seats than everything else on this page.

### OBC-NCL: the validity window

The non-creamy-layer certificate must be current per the counselling authority's requirement. An expired NCL certificate is an invalid NCL certificate.

## Registration: Step by Step

**On `mcc.nic.in`:**

1. **Register** with your NEET application number and roll number
2. **Fill personal details** — these must match your NEET application exactly
3. **Pay the registration fee** — non-refundable, varies by category and seat type
4. **Pay the security deposit** — refundable, but forfeited if you violate round rules
5. **Fill choices**
6. **Lock choices**
7. Await allotment

**Errors that break registration:**
- Name mismatch with your NEET application → fix with NTA first, not MCC
- Wrong category selected during registration → some fields can't be edited after payment
- Payment succeeds but doesn't reflect → wait 24h, then raise a grievance with the transaction ID. Do not pay twice.

**Take screenshots at every step.** Registration confirmation, fee payment, locked choice list. The MCC portal has a well-earned reputation for load problems during peak windows, and a screenshot is your only evidence.

## Choice Filling: The Strategy That Wins Seats

This is where the entire process is decided. Read this section twice.

### The one principle

**Order by genuine preference. Not by probability.**

The algorithm processes your list top-down and gives you the **highest choice you are eligible for**. If your rank doesn't reach choice #1, it moves to #2. Then #3. It does not penalise you, downgrade you, or notice that you were ambitious.

**There is zero cost to optimism at the top of your list.**

**The cost is entirely at the bottom.** Every college you don't list is a seat you cannot be allotted.

### How to build it

1. Run a prediction and sort the output into **reach / match / safe** buckets — [do that here](/neet)
2. List your **reach** colleges first, in true preference order
3. Then **match**
4. Then **safe**
5. **List everything you would genuinely attend.** A 200-choice list costs you nothing but an hour.

### The two failure modes

**Too short.** Students list 15 choices, get nothing in Round 1, and watch options fill in Round 2 that they'd have taken happily. If you have 150 acceptable colleges, list 150.

**Listing something you won't join.** If you would not attend a college, **do not list it.** Being allotted a seat you then abandon carries consequences — forfeited security deposit, and in some rounds, debarment from subsequent rounds. And in the stray round, it isn't optional at all.

### The government-vs-private ordering

A government MBBS seat at a mid-tier college beats a private MBBS seat at a "better" one on almost every axis that matters:

| | Government MBBS | Private MBBS |
|---|---|---|
| Total fee | ₹6,000 – ₹5 lakh | ₹60 lakh – ₹1.2 crore |
| Financed cost (11%, 10yr) | Negligible | **~₹1.5 crore repaid** |
| Internship stipend | ₹15,000 – ₹30,000/month | Often minimal |
| Patient volume | High — this is the curriculum | Often thin |

Order accordingly, unless you have a specific reason not to. And "the campus looked nicer" is not a ₹1.5 crore reason.

## Locking: Fill Is Not Submit

**A filled list is not a submitted list.**

Most portals auto-lock at the deadline. **Do not rely on that.** Some don't, and an unlocked list is an unprocessed list — which means no allotment, in a round you paid for.

**Do this:**
1. Lock explicitly
2. Screenshot the confirmation
3. Download the locked choice list PDF
4. Then close the tab and stop looking at it

You cannot change a locked list. Four days of second-guessing it changes nothing except your sleep.

## Seat Allotment and the Algorithm

The algorithm is a straightforward serial dictatorship:

1. Sort all candidates by rank — best first
2. For each candidate, walk their choice list top-down
3. Allot the first choice with a seat available in their eligible category
4. Remove that seat from the pool
5. Next candidate

**Two properties worth understanding:**

**It's not a lottery.** Given everyone's rank and everyone's choice list, the outcome is fully determined. There is no luck in it and nothing to pray about after locking.

**Your result depends on other people's choice lists**, which is why closing ranks move year to year even at a fixed seat count. If a college becomes fashionable, its closing rank tightens — not because it got better, but because better ranks listed it higher.

**Results publish on the portal.** You'll see an allotted seat or "not allotted."

## Free Exit, Upgrade and Resignation

Your options after an allotment:

| Option | What happens | Deposit |
|---|---|---|
| **Accept and report** | You join. Out of subsequent rounds. | Refunded per rules |
| **Free exit** (Round 1 only) | Surrender the seat, participate in Round 2 | **Refunded** |
| **Upgrade** | Report but opt for upgrade next round. **Rules vary by round — read the bulletin.** | Held |
| **Do nothing** | Seat forfeited, deposit forfeited, possible debarment | **Forfeited** |

**"Do nothing" is never the answer.** If you don't want the seat, take the free exit explicitly. Silence is the most expensive option on this table.

**On upgrade:** the rules genuinely change between years and rounds. Whether you can upgrade, whether you must report first, whether your existing seat is held — all of it is specified in that round's information bulletin and nowhere else. Read it. This article is not a substitute and neither is your senior.

## The Stray Vacancy Round: Read This Twice

**The stray vacancy round has no exit provision. If you are allotted a seat, you must join. Resignation is not permitted.**

**Why it's tempting:** closing ranks are widest here — sometimes dramatically. Seats appear that were unreachable in Round 1.

**Why it's dangerous:** those wide ranks are attached to seats nobody wanted. Some of them are at expensive deemed universities. If you list a ₹1.2 crore deemed seat "just in case" and get allotted, you are joining, or you are forfeiting your deposit and absorbing whatever debarment applies.

**The rule, and it's simple:**

> **Only list choices in the stray round that you will genuinely accept, at the fee they carry, if allotted.**

Not "probably." Not "if nothing better comes." There is nothing better coming. The stray round is the last round.

**Before you fill it:** open your locked list, go line by line, and for each one ask *"if this is my allotment, am I joining?"* Delete every line where the answer isn't yes.

## Security Deposit and Fees

| Payment | Refundable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Registration fee | **No** | Varies by category and seat type |
| Security deposit | **Yes** — if you follow the rules | Forfeited on violation |
| College fee | At the college | Paid on reporting |

**The security deposit is a behavioural instrument, not a revenue instrument.** It exists to stop candidates blocking seats they don't want. Free exit in Round 1 returns it. Abandoning a seat forfeits it.

**Deemed university deposits are substantially higher** than government ones — which is a deliberate filter, and worth knowing before you casually add deemed choices to a list.

**Refund timelines are slow.** Budget for the money being gone for a while.

## Document Verification and Reporting

Report to the allotted college with:
- Provisional allotment letter
- All originals from the document list
- Three photocopy sets
- Fee payment as specified

### The reporting deadline is absolute

Not "usually flexible." Absolute. Miss it and your seat returns to the pool for the next round. There is no appeal, and "my train was cancelled" is not a category MCC recognises.

**Travel a day early.** For a decision this size, a hotel night is not an expense.

### On your original documents

Colleges verify and return. **Some ask to retain originals until course completion.** This practice has been directed against by regulators, and you are within your rights to ask for written justification and a receipt.

**Practical advice:** don't turn it into a confrontation on day one. Ask politely, get a receipt, and escalate through proper channels afterwards if needed. You have five years at this institution.

## State Counselling: What's Different

There is no single national state-counselling portal. Each state runs its own.

**Find yours:** search "[Your State] NEET UG counselling 2026 official" and **verify the domain is `.gov.in` or `.nic.in`** before entering anything.

### What differs from MCC

| Element | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Domicile** | Required. Rules vary sharply — 7 years' schooling, parent's domicile, Class 10+12 from the state, or combinations |
| **Category certificate** | **State format**, not central |
| **Merit list** | State's own, derived from NEET marks |
| **Rounds** | Usually mirror MCC's structure, but not the dates |
| **Special quotas** | State-specific — government school quota, defence wards, regional quotas |
| **Bond** | **Many states impose a compulsory rural service bond after MBBS** |

### The bond

This is the thing students discover after joining.

| Element | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Service period | 1 – 5 years |
| Penalty for breaking | ₹5 lakh – ₹40 lakh+ |
| Enforcement | Varies; some states withhold the degree certificate |

A 5-year bond with a ₹40 lakh penalty materially changes what a "free" government seat costs you — especially if you're planning NEET PG straight after internship.

**Read the bond terms in your state's information bulletin before you lock choices.** Not after allotment.

### Managing two calendars

You will be running MCC and state counselling simultaneously, on different dates, with different documents. Put both on your phone with alerts. This is the most common place organised students beat higher-ranked disorganised ones.

## AYUSH Counselling Through AACCC

BAMS, BHMS, BUMS and BSMS run through the **AYUSH Admissions Central Counselling Committee (AACCC)** at `aaccc.gov.in` for the All India Quota, and through state authorities for state quota.

**Separate process. Separate registration. Separate portal.** It is not part of MCC.

**The honest framing:** AYUSH is not a consolation prize, and it is not equivalent to MBBS. It's a distinct professional path with real government employment, a growing sector, and genuinely lower fees than private MBBS. Some students who take it are delighted. Some are miserable because they took it while still wanting MBBS.

**The question worth answering honestly:** do you want *this*, or do you want MBBS and this is what's available? Both answers are legitimate. Only one of them should lead to registering.

## BSc Nursing and BVSc Counselling

**BSc Nursing** — MCC conducts counselling for central institutions. State authorities for the rest. Strong employability, and a genuinely strong overseas pathway that is underrated in Indian family conversations about medicine.

**BVSc (Veterinary)** — through the VCI quota via MCC, plus state counselling.

Both have closing ranks substantially wider than MBBS. Both are real careers. Neither is a failure.

## Fraud: How the Scams Actually Work

NTA has issued an explicit warning about fraudulent calls, fake admission offers and sites claiming guaranteed seats. This warning is annual because the fraud is industrial and it targets students in exactly this window, at exactly the moment they're most frightened.

**Here is how it actually works, so you recognise it:**

| The pitch | The reality |
|---|---|
| "Management quota seat available, pay to book" | There is no bookable management quota in NEET. Allotment is algorithmic. |
| "We'll fill your choices for you — share your login" | They lock a list you didn't choose, often at a college paying them a commission. |
| "Call from MCC — pending verification fee" | MCC does not call candidates. Ever. |
| "Guaranteed admission or full refund" | The refund does not exist. Neither does the guarantee. |
| "Special quota, limited seats, decide today" | Urgency is the tell. Every real deadline is published on a government site. |

**The hard rules:**

- **No one can guarantee you a seat.** Not for money. Allotment is algorithmic and rank-based.
- **Never share your NEET or MCC login.** Not with a coaching institute, not with a "counsellor," not with anyone.
- **MCC and NTA do not call you asking for money.**
- **Only trust:** `neet.nta.nic.in` · `mcc.nic.in` · `aaccc.gov.in` · your official state portal

**And a word about us:** legitimate counselling guidance — including College Parichay's — helps you understand your options, read the seat matrix, and build a good choice list. It does not, and cannot, get you a seat your rank does not earn. **Anyone telling you otherwise, including anyone claiming to be us, is lying to you.**

### When will NEET counselling 2026 begin?
MCC has not announced the Round 1 registration date. Monitor `mcc.nic.in` daily — there are no SMS reminders and the window is typically under a week without extension.

### Do I need to register separately for MCC and state counselling?
Yes. They are entirely independent processes with separate portals, fees and deadlines. Registering for one does not register you for the other.

### What is the difference between AIQ and state quota?
AIQ (via MCC) covers 15% of state government seats plus 100% of central institutions and deemed universities — roughly 8,000 government MBBS seats. State quota covers the remaining 85% plus state private colleges — roughly 48,000 seats — and requires domicile.

### Which category certificate do I need for AIQ counselling?
The **central government prescribed format**. A state-format certificate is valid for state counselling and invalid for AIQ. If you only hold the state one, apply for the central one immediately — it takes two to four weeks.

### My EWS certificate is from FY 2024–25. Is it valid?
No. It must be issued for **FY 2025–26** in central format. An invalid EWS certificate collapses your claim to General mid-counselling.

### How many choices should I fill?
Every college you would genuinely attend, in true preference order. A long list costs nothing. A short list costs seats.

### Will listing an ambitious college first hurt my chances?
No. The algorithm gives you the highest choice you're eligible for and moves down your list otherwise. There is no penalty for optimism at the top.

### What is free exit in MCC counselling?
In Round 1 only, you may surrender an allotted seat with your security deposit refunded and participate in Round 2. It does not exist in the stray vacancy round.

### Can I resign a seat allotted in the stray vacancy round?
No. The stray round has no exit provision — allotment means mandatory joining. Only list choices you will genuinely accept at the fee they carry.

### What happens if I don't report to my allotted college?
Your seat is forfeited, your security deposit is forfeited, and depending on the round you may be debarred from subsequent rounds. If you don't want a seat, take the free exit explicitly. Never stay silent.

### Do closing ranks go down in later rounds?
Yes. Round 1 is tightest; ranks loosen through Rounds 2, 3 and stray as candidates vacate seats. Use MCC's published Round 1 closing ranks to recalibrate your Round 2 list — that's this year's real data.

### Is there a bond for government medical colleges?
Many state colleges impose a compulsory rural service bond of 1–5 years with penalties from ₹5 lakh to ₹40 lakh+. Terms vary sharply by state. Read your state's information bulletin before locking choices.

### What is the ESIC IP quota?
A quota at ESIC medical colleges for children of ESIC-insured workers, with substantially wider closing ranks than open ESIC seats. Ask your parents whether their employer deducts ESIC — lakhs of families are eligible without realising it.

### How do I apply for AYUSH courses?
Through **AACCC** at `aaccc.gov.in` for All India Quota, and through state authorities for state quota. It is a completely separate process from MCC.

### Can anyone guarantee me a seat through management quota?
No. NTA has explicitly warned against this. Allotment is algorithmic and rank-based. Anyone guaranteeing a seat for money — including anyone claiming to represent us — is running a fraud.

### Why do I need to read the seat matrix?
It shows exactly how many seats exist in your category at each college, and which colleges are **new this year**. New colleges have no closing-rank history, so no predictor covers them and most students skip them — which is exactly why they close wide.
